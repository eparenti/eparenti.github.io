How continuo works

Continuo is an advisor with guardrails for people who build with AI. It runs on your machine beside the AI assistants you already use, checks what they claim against evidence, measures quality, cost, and speed, and learns which ways of working pay off for you.

At a glance

Three layers sit inside continuo. Packages are the features, each doing one job an engineering organization does for its builders. Services are what every package shares: the learning loop, the advisor, the meter, the model runner, the decision queue, the sandbox, and the publish gate. The core holds the shared records, the verdict rules, and the store, and makes no model calls.

The problem

AI made producing work cheap, but checking it, steering it, and keeping up with it did not get cheaper. In a company, an engineering organization does that work: QA, code review, release gates, project tracking, budget control, security, and retrospectives. A person building alone with AI has none of it.

So they either slow down to check everything by hand, or ship work nobody checked. Their assistants say "done" when tests never ran. Costs drift without anyone noticing. Lessons learned in one session are gone in the next. Continuo gives that person the parts of an engineering organization that keep work honest, cheap, and improving.

Your day with continuo

You start and end work on one local page. Your assistants keep working in your terminal or editor as before; the page shows what they did and what needs you.

You answer a decision on the page and the answer reaches the agent that asked. You never copy text between windows to keep projects moving.

Claimed against proven

Continuo never reports work as done, fixed, or better without proof. "Claimed" means an agent said it is done. "Proven" means a script confirmed it. The page always shows the two apart.

Agents report their work by writing a small status file in each project, .continuo/status.toml: items, their state, the evidence for done items, and questions for you. Continuo reads it, checks each piece of evidence, and records the result in shared records that every feature uses.

RecordWhat it holds
ProjectA repo or body of work you registered, with its realm (personal, work, or sensitive)
FindingOne checked claim: what was claimed, the evidence, and the verdict
DecisionA question waiting on you, or one you answered, tied to the exact wording you read
ReceiptProof that a step happened: a test run, a check, a gate, a review, bound to a commit
CardOne practice your agents follow, with its state and the evidence for it

The verdict rules are fixed and run without any model:

  1. Verified needs at least one piece of evidence a script confirmed, and every check the claim cites must have passed on the current code.
  2. A timeout, error, crash, or missing result never verifies. It goes to review.
  3. Rejected needs evidence that contradicts the claim. Without it, the claim goes to review.
  4. You can override any verdict. The override is recorded with who and when.

A pass from before the latest changes does not count. If the code moves after a check passed, the item drops back to "claimed" until the check runs again.

How it learns how you work

Continuo turns what you notice while working into practices your agents follow, then checks whether those practices help. The loop is: measure a stretch of work, judge it, remember what mattered, change how agents are told to work, and check whether the change helped.

Marks
While you work, one gesture records "good" or "waste", with an optional word. A mark comes from the page, the command line, or a skill inside your assistant.
Stretches and checkpoints
A stretch is the work between two checkpoints: two hours on one project or three days across four. At a checkpoint, continuo shows the facts it measured (projects touched, spend, sessions, commits and merges, checks passed or missing, waste signals, your marks), and you give a short verdict. Every field is optional, and a checkpoint never stops work.
Practice cards
A card is one practice, written as an instruction an agent can follow and a person can check, such as "run the full test suite and gate on its exit code before each commit". Cards come from your marks and checkpoint notes, from the advisor, and from the industry.
Rendering
Cards on trial or proven are written into the files every assistant reads (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, Cursor rules, and others) through Rulesync, next to the rules you wrote by hand. Nothing is rendered into a repo until you say so.
Compliance
Where a card can be checked by a script, continuo checks whether agents followed it on real work: commit message format, a forbidden character in changed lines, a passing check at every commit. A proven card followed 30% of the time is shown as a dead letter.

A trial lasts ten judged stretches or forty active agent hours, whichever comes first. A retired card keeps its history, so the same idea is not proposed again as new.

No number promotes a card. The page shows compliance, the trend, and your own marks for the trial, and you decide. Every rendered card costs tokens on every turn, so the page also shows what each card costs to carry.

The advisor

The advisor studies your work and adapts continuo's advice to you. It looks at verdicts, the decisions you took and refused, your marks and checkpoint notes, where work went wrong, and what it cost.

It runs inside the assistant you already use. Continuo writes a brief from its records; a continuo skill in your assistant reads it and writes back proposals. There are no hidden model calls and no spend outside your own subscription. Every piece of advice says what it rests on.

The advisor mayExample
Change on its own, visibly and reversiblyThe order of what the page shows first; stop repeating a reminder you dismissed three times
Propose, for you to approveA new practice card; a change to how work is routed to models; a change to a guardrail
NeverSet a verdict, decide a route, or relax a guardrail

Every change it makes on its own is listed on the page with an undo button.

Practices from the industry

Continuo brings in what other teams have learned about building with AI, so your practice base is never only your own habits. It reads an allowlist of trusted sources: vendor engineering guides and cookbooks, tool changelogs, the MCP specification, and a small set of high-signal team writeups. You can also feed in a link by hand.

Each item must answer one question: should this change how we design, build, test, or run agents? If yes, it becomes a candidate card tagged with its source. It skips what you already use, learns from what you keep and drop, and stops reading sources that never produce anything you keep.

Outside text is treated as data, never as an instruction. A world card goes through the same election as your own cards and becomes proven only on evidence from your own work, by your promotion. When it contradicts one of your cards, continuo shows the conflict as a question.

Guardrails

Most of continuo advises, and you decide. A few parts stop a mistake before it happens, because the mistake is costly or cannot be undone.

GuardrailWhat it stops
BudgetsA job that goes over its cost cap
SandboxA worker reaching your credentials, your other files, or the open network; workers reach only their model's API through an allowlist
Publish gatePushes, merges, and releases outside your rules: wrong account, a secret in the diff, scope beyond the task, or missing evidence

Workers that continuo starts on your behalf produce branches and diffs locally and never hold your git credentials. Publishing happens only from your own session, after the gate's checks. A new guardrail needs a reason of the same kind; everything else stays advice.

Packages

Each feature is a package that does one job a good engineering organization does for its builders. Every package runs on its own, reads and writes the shared records, calls models only through continuo's model runner, and respects realms. Some are native to continuo; others are adapters that connect a tool you already use and keep that tool's own identity.

Better, cheaper, faster

Each word maps to a number continuo records. A claim of improvement without the number is not made.

WordMeasured asFrom
BetterChecks passed, findings verified, rework rate, card complianceVerdicts, receipts, checkpoints
CheaperCost per verified task, seat use, cost of rendered rulesThe meter, which prices every model call it can see
FasterTime to a verified outcomeThe meter, task records

The order is fixed: cheaper and faster never come at the cost of better. Continuo routes each task to the cheapest model that can do it well, but never lowers the requested scope, security, evidence, or quality bar to save money.

The trend view shows each number over your last judged stretches, plus an honesty line: how many stretches went unjudged, which sessions were only partly measured, and how thin the evidence is. Continuo never shows a before-and-after average as proof that a practice caused a change. If quality and cost stay flat for twenty judged stretches, it says so.

Your data

Continuo runs on your machine. Your work, transcripts, and records stay local, and nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose it.

Numbers only
The store keeps counts, ids, costs, verdicts, and the short text your agents wrote into status files. It never stores prompts, responses, or file contents from your sessions.
Realms
Each project is personal, work, or sensitive. Material from different realms never mixes in one brief, one task, or one report, and rendering into work repos is off unless you opt a repo in.
A scrubbed practice base
Cards never contain project names, repo names, paths, hostnames, costs, or transcript text. A check blocks any commit to your practice base that does, using a denylist that stays on your machine. Evidence for a card crosses that line only as counts and dates.
Private by default
Your practice cards live where you choose: on this machine, or in a git repo you own. Publishing any of it is always your decision.
No scoring of people
What continuo learns about you serves only you. It is never reported or ranked.

The page listens only on this machine, checks every request for a per-start token, and refuses requests from other sites.

Getting started and configuration

Continuo is open source and installs with one command. A first run takes a few minutes and needs no hand-editing.

  1. Install. uv tool install continuo or pipx install continuo. The standard packages come with it; others install the same way.
  2. Set up. continuo init finds the assistants on your machine (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini, OpenCode) and the tools continuo can use, and shows what each one enables. It asks a few questions, each with a default: which project to start with and its realm, where your practice cards live, and whether to render cards into this repo. It shows every file it would change before changing it.
  3. Teach your agents. Continuo adds a short guide to your agents' instructions so they keep the project's status file current and ask you questions through it.
  4. Open the page. continuo page opens it in your browser. Your project and its plan show up right away; checks, costs, and cards fill in as you work.
uv tool install continuo
continuo init
continuo page
continuo doctor      # each assistant: measured, guided, rendered
continuo off         # pause everything, files left in place
continuo uninstall   # remove hooks and settings, keep records and cards

All settings live in one file in continuo's home folder, readable and checked strictly: a mistake gets a plain message that says what is wrong and how to fix it. continuo doctor shows a table of each assistant on this machine and whether it is measured, guided, and rendered. continuo off pauses everything and leaves your files in place; continuo uninstall removes hooks and settings and leaves your records and cards for you to keep or delete.

What continuo is not

Not a coding assistant
It does not build for you. Your assistants build; continuo surrounds them with checks, measurement, and memory.
Not a replacement for your tools
It works with the assistants, issue trackers, and check runners you already use, and reads them through adapters.
Not an agent orchestrator
Tools that run fleets of agents for you are tools continuo can read. It does not compete with them.
Not a cloud service or a team chat tool
It runs locally for one builder.
Not self-modifying
Nothing promotes itself. Every practice, route, and guardrail change that matters waits for you.
